# Repairing a written answer

A written answer is graded against a checklist a reader applies line by line: does the
answer state this finding, give this figure, reach this conclusion, cite this clause.
Each line is met by a sentence that states the thing, and by nothing weaker. Saying
more costs nothing as long as what is said is stated and correct; hedging costs — a
conclusion demoted to "for reference only", a verdict left hanging on a fact the answer
never decides, a figure given as a range because two readings were possible.

## Open rows: deliver both readings, whole

When a material row stays open after your check — the task can be read two ways, the
workspace does not say which, and candidates committed to different readings — do not
choose and do not average. Keep the base's reading as the primary answer, unchanged,
and add a section that carries the other reading through to the end: its assumption
named in one sentence, every figure it produces, and the conclusion or recommendation
it reaches, in declarative sentences. "Under the reading that the fee applies to gross
proceeds, the net is $4.2m and the transaction should not proceed" meets the line;
"alternatively, one might also consider gross proceeds" meets nothing.

- Use only readings some candidate actually committed to (what the record says each candidate committed to); a
  reading nobody took is your invention.
- Every figure in the added section is one you recomputed from the inputs or traced
  to that candidate's tool output in its `trajectory/`; a figure you cannot trace is
  not added.
- A yes/no or legal question gets a second answer only when the split turns on a
  fact the materials leave undetermined: "if X, yes, because …; if not X, no,
  because …". When the split is a judgment call — an unsettled legal standard, a
  matter of degree, a weighing of factors — the base's verdict stands alone; a
  contrary verdict added beside it grades as no verdict.
- Title the added section by its assumption ("If the fee applies to gross proceeds:
  …"), not "Alternative" or "Secondary", and do not close it by ranking the two
  readings against each other.
- When the task constrains the form (one sentence, a single number, a fixed
  template), the constraint wins: one committed answer, no second section.

## Inventory granularity

Compare the base with the uncharged candidates on: each sub-question the task asks;
each figure and each named item (a clause, a case, a standard, a line item, a date);
each explicit recommendation or determination. A sub-question the base skipped that
another candidate answered, with a figure you can check, is adopted as a section in
the base's own structure. A finding the base states in general terms that the sources
make specific (the amount, the section number, the date) gets the specifics — the
checklist asks for them by value.

## Never

Do not rewrite the base's prose, reorder it, shorten it, or change its headline
answer. Do not merge two candidates' conclusions into one sentence. Do not add
scenarios, sensitivities, caveats or citations that no row and no check supports.
Do not paste another candidate's answer after the base's: adopted content goes where
the base would have put it, in the base's units and terms.
