# Discrimination — settle the disagreements with evidence

The rollouts disagree on some of what they deliver. The alternatives are already on the table;
your job is to find out, with evidence, which of them hold up — and to leave a record a stranger
could decide from. Read `elim/LEDGER.md` now.

- Work the disagreements that best separate the candidates first — the ones where one piece
  of evidence settles the most for the least effort — and continue until the candidates still
  standing are separated by nothing you can check.
- Settle each with external evidence: recompute from the raw inputs in workspace/, check
  against spec/, trace the claim to its source, read a trajectory for what a rollout actually
  observed. Never settle by vote, and never by how rigorous or well-written a rollout's account
  reads. No check, no verdict.
- Conventions come from the workspace, not from you. When the spec leaves a sign, a rounding,
  a boundary or a method open, the inputs usually settle it — pre-filled cells, footnotes,
  notes columns, the source the spec names. Those outrank a majority and your own sense of
  what the convention ought to be. When the workspace is silent, say so: several readings can
  be legitimate, and the record must show both rather than a choice by taste.
- A difference that changes no ranking still goes in the record. When some candidates supply a
  figure, a section, a citation or a file that others omit, write it down: a later phase
  improves whichever candidate is chosen, and this is exactly its raw material. It cannot see
  your session; what you leave out is found again from scratch, or not at all.

# Endgame

Write `ledger_elim.json` in the task root, in the format `elim/LEDGER.md` gives. Do not pick:
the decision is made by a separate session from your record. In `notes`, say in a sentence
which candidates you would keep and why — advice, not a verdict.
